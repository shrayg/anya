# Anya Meta ads and funnel operating system

Status: operating draft for launch planning  
Research and competitive audit date: 2026-08-24  
Primary market assumed: United States  
Scope: paid Meta acquisition for Anya's current single-frame campaigns, followed by landing, search, account, checkout, and retention

This is both a human decision guide and an assistant handoff. Treat budgets, target costs, and winning creative claims as hypotheses until Anya has its own purchase, retention, refund, and incrementality data.

## 0. Executive funnel theory

### The central decision

Anya should not copy Snoop.city's funnel literally and should not describe its strategy as exploiting the sunk-cost fallacy.

Snoop's best mechanism is progressive relevance: each answer makes the next screen feel more specific to the visitor's situation. Its riskiest mechanism is emotional escalation: the copy repeatedly validates suspicion, a simulated search reports matches before the user sees substantiated evidence, and recurring offers appear only after a long sequence of emotionally loaded questions.

Anya's stronger model is:

> One clue becomes a truthful, increasingly relevant search plan; a real search produces a real preview; the customer pays to reveal, save, or continue the work.

The commitment comes from useful progress, not pressure. The visitor should be able to explain what Anya has done, what remains locked, what they are buying, and whether the charge repeats.

### The five operating principles

1. **Message match is the first conversion tool.** Every ad must land on a page that repeats its exact situation, starting clue, vocabulary, and product proof. Generic home and pricing pages are not acceptable paid destinations.
2. **The product must earn the paywall.** Run a real search or real source-availability check before showing a result count. Never claim MATCH, FOUND, active, cheating, dangerous, or exposed unless the underlying response supports that exact statement.
3. **The funnel asks only questions that change the search or the page.** If an answer does not change routing, source selection, copy, or offer, remove the question.
4. **Emotion wins the stop; evidence wins the purchase.** Face-led cheating creative is a high-priority hypothesis because it creates immediate human tension. The next screen must transition quickly to Anya's input-to-result proof.
5. **Optimize for profitable retained customers, not cheap clicks or forced checkouts.** The account scorecard includes paid conversion, contribution margin, refunds, chargebacks, cancellations, negative feedback, and repeat search behavior.

### What behavioral science actually supports

Sunk cost is the tendency to continue because resources have already been spent, even when continuing is not the best forward-looking choice. That is not a healthy product principle. Three adjacent effects are more useful and defensible:

- **Endowed progress:** perceived initial progress can increase completion effort. Anya can truthfully show that the ad has preselected a search path or that a valid clue has been recognized.
- **Goal gradient:** motivation can increase as a person approaches a visible goal. Use an honest progress indicator tied to actual required steps.
- **Commitment and consistency:** a small, freely chosen action can increase follow-through. Anya should use reversible one-tap choices, not trap visitors with emotional admissions.

Practical rule: progress must represent work actually completed. Never invent source scans, matches, timers, scarcity, or personalized conclusions.

### Recommended shared funnel shape

Cold paid traffic should usually see five to eight meaningful screens, not Snoop's roughly twenty-question interrogation.

1. **Ad-matched landing:** exact hook, one-sentence product explanation, real Anya logo, and the starting clue already selected.
2. **Clue input:** phone, email, username, Discord ID, or name. Keep sensitive values in request bodies, not URLs.
3. **One routing question:** context or source scope; only ask it when the answer changes the search.
4. **Search preparation:** state what Anya will check and the limits of the result. Include lawful-use acknowledgement where appropriate.
5. **Truthful live search:** actual progress from the application, or a neutral loading state without fabricated source counts.
6. **Real preview:** actual number of available result groups and non-sensitive category labels; clearly mark unavailable sources.
7. **Save or reveal:** ask the visitor to create an account or provide an email to preserve the real result. Explain retention and privacy.
8. **Offer:** one-time search option first for cold consumer traffic, then clearly labeled recurring Starter plan. Show price, billing cadence, renewal, cancellation, and exact entitlement beside the button.

The self-exposure funnel should be shorter because the user can safely enter their own email immediately. Older audiences should also get fewer steps, larger type, explicit labels, and no youth slang.

## 2. Snoop.city competitive audit

### Audit boundaries

Observed on 2026-08-24 in the United States from the public /go path and public Ad Library. No purchase was made and no email was submitted. Ad Library shows public creative and dates; it does not expose Snoop's complete targeting, spend, conversion rate, or profitability.

### Active ad pattern

Seven active Snoop.city ads were visible during the audit. All sent traffic to https://snoop.city/go. The creative territory was unusually concentrated:

- Male partner betrayal.
- Snapchat or Discord activity.
- Specific late-night time stamps.
- First-person, testimonial-like story copy.
- Repeated headline: “snoop them before you trust them.”
- Repeated promise: “search anyone. see what they're hiding.”

The longest-running visible ad started 2026-07-22; several others began between 2026-08-20 and 2026-08-21. Longevity is a weak performance signal, not proof of profitability.

### Observed funnel sequence

The “my partner” path used approximately this sequence:

1. First name.
2. Last name.
3. Relationship type.
4. Relationship duration.
5. Location.
6. Reason for looking.
7. Age.
8. Suspected platforms.
9. Behavior signals.
10. Timing of suspicious moments.
11. Previous checking effort.
12. Prior accuracy of the visitor's instincts.
13. Emotional cost.
14. Degree of certainty.
15. Desired outcome.
16. Optional photo.
17. Required phone or email.
18. Simulated search theater with source counts and MATCH/FOUND states.
19. Email gate.
20. Paywall loaded after email.

The observed paywall code exposed one-time and recurring offers, including a $9.99 one-time report, weekly, monthly, three-month, six-month, and yearly plans. Some traffic branches appeared to receive different offer sets and recovery discounts. The funnel persisted UTM/campaign/ad identifiers and used Meta Pixel plus product analytics events.

#### Observed attribution and event design

- UTM source, medium, campaign, content, and term were persisted along with campaign/ad-set/ad IDs and names.
- Meta Pixel and PostHog were present.
- Product event names visible in client code included quiz completion to theater, delivery email submitted, and plan selected.
- A Meta Lead event was associated with quiz completion into the theater rather than a completed purchase.
- Email submission saved the report and then lazy-loaded the paywall.

Anya should keep the strong campaign continuity and explicit event taxonomy, but it should optimize toward confirmed economic events and avoid training Meta primarily on emotionally engaged quiz completion.

### Where Snoop shines

1. **Excellent narrative continuity.** The ad's late-night betrayal moment becomes the first funnel story rather than dumping the visitor on a generic search form.
2. **Low interaction cost per screen.** Most screens have one question and large tap targets.
3. **First-name personalization.** Reusing the subject's name makes later screens feel tailored.
4. **Concrete moments.** “1:12 AM,” phone face-down, and unknown contact are easier to imagine than abstract “relationship uncertainty.”
5. **Progressive commitment.** The visitor builds a case description one choice at a time.
6. **Offer breadth.** A one-time report lowers commitment while subscriptions monetize repeated use.
7. **Campaign continuity.** Every active ad has a dedicated-feeling path even though all use one URL.

### Where Snoop likely loses customers

1. **Excessive length.** Roughly twenty interactions before value is a large mobile abandonment surface, especially for older visitors.
2. **Questions that do not visibly improve the search.** Emotional-cost and gut-accuracy questions feel like persuasion rather than product configuration.
3. **Confirmation bias.** Lines that suggest the visitor's feeling is usually right validate guilt before evidence.
4. **Trust-breaking search theater.** Showing MATCH or FOUND during a simulated scan can feel deceptive if a real source response has not produced it.
5. **Privacy anxiety.** The funnel collects a name, location, relationship context, behavior, age, contact information, and optional photo before proving value.
6. **Claim inconsistency.** Different surfaces referenced 150+ apps, 640 sources, and 1,000+ sources. Scope inflation weakens credibility.
7. **Late recoverability.** Contact capture comes after many steps, so early abandoners cannot receive a legitimate saved-search reminder.
8. **Subscription risk.** Weekly billing and recovery timers can lift immediate revenue while increasing cancellation, refund, and chargeback pressure.
9. **Narrow audience story.** The current ad set leaves self-protection, parent safety, first-date verification, marketplace safety, and identity mismatch underdeveloped.
10. **Little visible evidence differentiation.** The ads promise secrets more strongly than they demonstrate a reviewable source trail.

### Anya's counter-position

| Snoop pattern                             | Anya response                                                      |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| “See what they're hiding”                 | “See what publicly connects to the clue.”                          |
| Long emotional interrogation              | Four to eight steps; every answer changes routing or copy          |
| Simulated source fireworks                | Actual search progress or neutral loading                          |
| Suspicion treated as evidence             | Identity signals labeled as clues, not conclusions                 |
| One generic /go URL with hidden branching | Explicit campaign and funnel IDs with exact ad-message continuity  |
| Repeated partner-betrayal story           | Four consumer categories plus professional separation              |
| Subscription after pressure               | One-time option first, recurring option clearly labeled            |
| Broad “hidden” promise                    | Visible source categories, timestamps, confidence, and limitations |

## 3. Anya's current conversion reality

### What already exists

The current application already has important pieces of a defensible paid funnel:

- Homepage search modes for email, phone, username, Discord, and breaches.
- Real streaming/search progress in the application.
- Server-side result vaulting with a 24-hour expiration.
- Blurred guest/free previews with an actual result count.
- Search resume state across authentication and checkout.
- Credit unlocks and recurring plans.
- A real Anya product UI that matches the campaign design language.

### High-priority conversion gaps

1. **No Meta Pixel or Conversions API implementation was found in the inspected app/components/lib/config paths.** The site visit beacon is first-party only. Paid launch should wait for verified browser and server purchase events.
2. **Generic destinations break message match.** Several campaign rows point to /#search or /pricing. Relationship dating and name-search trust ads go directly to pricing rather than proving the promised result.
3. **Checkout requires authentication.** Cold visitors must create an account before Square/OxaPay checkout. This may be acceptable, but it needs explicit measurement and an account-first versus email-first test.
4. **The one-time offer is hidden inside credit mechanics.** “Buy custom credits” is investigator language. A consumer should see “Reveal this search — one-time” with exact searches included.
5. **Name Search is plan-gated.** Babysitter, coach, and home-entry campaigns currently promise a workflow that is not available as a simple consumer one-time search.
6. **Babysitter and coach positioning creates FCRA risk.** Marketing compiled information for a hiring or eligibility decision can create obligations that a disclaimer alone cannot erase. These ads need counsel and product-purpose controls before paid launch.
7. **No campaign-level landing routes exist.** Add a stable /go/[funnel] shell or equivalent that accepts opaque campaign metadata, not subject PII.

### Recommended product offer decision before launch

Build and test two transparent offers:

- **One-time Search Pass:** three reveals or one full case/report for a clearly stated one-time price. A proposed first test is $4.99–$9.99, but finance must calculate provider fees, source costs, support, refund reserve, and desired contribution before choosing the price.
- **Starter:** $14.99/month, recurring, 150 searches/day, core identity inputs. Display “renews monthly until canceled” next to the CTA and make cancellation simple.

Do not manufacture a crossed-out price without a real, substantiated prior selling price. Do not use a fake countdown. Do not hide the monthly cadence below the fold.

## 4. Meta account and business setup

### Ownership and security

- Create or confirm one Anya Meta Business Portfolio owned by the company.
- Add the official Anya Facebook Page and professional Instagram account.
- Create or claim the correct ad account with USD and America/New_York set correctly; currency/time zone changes are difficult later.
- Give named people least-privilege access. Keep two trusted admins to avoid a single point of failure.
- Require two-factor authentication for everyone with business access.
- Verify the business and the anyaint.com domain where Meta makes those controls available.
- Add at least two valid payment methods and set account-spend alerts, not artificially low caps that interrupt learning.
- Record the Business ID, ad account ID, Page ID, Instagram ID, dataset/Pixel ID, domain owner, billing owner, and emergency contact in the private operations vault.
- Do not share personal login credentials. Use partner/business access for agencies.

### Page readiness

- Use the real Anya logo and approved cover image.
- Complete About, website, privacy policy, terms, refund policy, contact email, and support path.
- Publish several non-ad posts that explain public-data lookup, privacy, lawful use, and product examples before scaling ads.
- Monitor comments daily. Hide doxxing, real identifiers, threats, and child information; answer good-faith product questions.
- Do not let testimonial-style ad copy appear to be a real customer's story unless it is real, permitted, and accurately disclosed.

### Dataset and domain checklist

- Create one production web dataset/Pixel for anyaint.com.
- Keep development and preview traffic out of production reporting.
- Configure browser Pixel and server Conversions API.
- Use the same event name and event ID for the same browser/server event so Meta can deduplicate it.
- Test all events in Events Manager before ads go live.
- Review event coverage, match quality, deduplication, diagnostics, and freshness every week during launch.
- Keep a source-of-truth event ledger in Anya's database for purchase, refund, renewal, and cancellation.

## 5. Privacy-first measurement architecture

### Non-negotiable data boundary

Never send the searched person's name, phone, email, username, Discord ID, age, location, photo, search result, suspected behavior, or relationship answer to Meta, PostHog, Google Analytics, a URL, UTM field, referrer, or ad-platform custom parameter.

Meta may receive the Anya customer's consented matching information where lawful and necessary for measurement. Subject-search data is different: it concerns another person and must remain inside Anya's protected application boundary.

Use only opaque IDs and coarse, non-sensitive taxonomy:

- funnel_id: REL_PHONE_V1
- campaign_id: HER-01
- creative_id: HER01_FACE_A_4X5
- step_id: clue_input
- clue_type: phone
- category: relationship
- result_bucket: 0, 1, 2_5, 6_plus
- offer_id: ONE_TIME_SEARCH_PASS
- experiment_id: PAYWALL_A

- Generate event_id on Anya's server or a shared client/server utility and pass the identical value to both channels.

## 6. Meta campaign architecture

### Objective and performance goal

Use the Sales objective for a paid consumer checkout. Optimize for Purchase once Purchase is trustworthy and receives enough volume to be useful. Do not run Traffic merely because clicks are cheaper; Meta optimizes for the requested outcome.

If purchase volume is initially too sparse:

1. Keep the Sales objective.
2. Use InitiateCheckout or a tightly defined qualified Lead only as a temporary performance goal.
3. Set a written migration gate before launch, such as enough weekly purchases for stable delivery and no duplicate-event diagnostics.
4. Move back to Purchase; do not allow cheap email capture to become the permanent business goal.

### Consolidation before segmentation

The existence of thirty ads does not justify launching thirty ads at once. At low spend, excessive campaigns and ad sets starve every cell.

Recommended launch structure:

- One controlled testing campaign using ad-set budgets while category allocation is being learned.
- Two to four active ad sets at a time.
- Three to five ads per ad set, with genuinely different concepts.
- One scaling campaign only after there are repeatable winners and enough spend to separate testing from delivery.
- A small high-intent recovery/retargeting campaign only after the site has a meaningful audience. Do not create a tiny retargeting cell that only harvests people who would have purchased anyway.

### Audience strategy

- United States only for the first controlled read.
- Adults 25+ for relationship and first-date creative; parents/trust commonly 30+ or 35+; data protection can be 30–65+.
- Start broad inside the meaningful age/gender boundary. Treat detailed interests as a test, not the default truth.
- Use Advantage+ audience suggestions when appropriate, but verify which fields are controls versus suggestions in the current interface. Meta's current Advantage+ behavior can expand beyond several suggestions.
- Separate gendered relationship creative only when the ad's wording and pictured fictional result require it. Never use race as a suspicion or risk signal.
- Exclude confirmed current customers from new-customer tests when the test question is acquisition. Use both customer-list and website audiences because each can be incomplete.
- Do not target minors. Parent-safety creative addresses adults.

### Placement strategy

- Begin with Advantage+ placements when each placement has a suitable asset.
- Upload native 4:5 feed, 1:1, and 9:16 Story/Reels assets. Do not rely on blind auto-crops for critical hook text or result cards.
- Keep the main hook inside safe zones and readable without zooming.
- Review every preview. Turn off a creative enhancement when it changes the identity, fictional result, claim, logo, layout, or meaning.
- The current single-frame library is suitable for feed tests. Produce 9:16 static adaptations now and native video later; do not stretch a 4:5 image.

### Attribution and reporting

- Start with the current default click/view attribution setting available for Sales, document it, and do not change it mid-test.
- Build decisions on server-confirmed purchases and blended CAC, not Ads Manager alone.
- Use A/B tests when changing one important variable. Use Conversion Lift once budget and conversion volume justify a holdout.
- Do not read one-day fluctuations as truth. Use planned checkpoints and confidence ranges.

## 7. Budget model

### No universal daily budget exists

The correct budget depends on the allowable customer acquisition cost, which depends on price, gross margin, search-provider cost, payment fees, refund/chargeback rate, support cost, and retained subscription value.

Calculate these before launch:

- One-time allowable CAC = price − payment fees − source cost − support allocation − refund/chargeback reserve − required contribution.
- Subscription allowable CAC = expected gross profit through the chosen payback window × acceptable acquisition share.
- Blended allowable CAC = mix-weighted one-time and subscription allowable CAC.

Use a conservative 60- or 90-day payback window until retention is proven. Do not value a new subscription as if it lasts forever.

For initial planning, allocate roughly three target CACs per active ad set per day and three to five target CACs of spend per concept before making a normal kill decision. These are planning heuristics, not Meta rules. Sparse or high-variance data may require more.

### Three launch scenarios

#### Lean: $100/day

- Run two ad sets at a time, not all four categories.
- Week 1: relationship and exposure/data.
- Week 2: parent safety and first-date/marketplace trust, while retaining only a clear Week 1 winner.
- Use three ads per ad set.
- Total 14-day media: $1,400.
- Expect directional learning, not a complete thirty-ad verdict.

#### Serious: $240/day

- Four active ad sets at approximately $60/day each.
- Relationship women, relationship men or general relationship, data protection, and a rotating kids/trust cell.
- Three to four ads per ad set.
- Total 14-day media: $3,360.
- This is the recommended planning case if target CAC is around $20 and the business can absorb the test.

#### Scale-ready: $500/day

- 70% proven prospecting/scaling, 20% controlled creative tests, up to 10% high-intent recovery once audience size supports it.
- Maintain category share based on marginal net CAC, not the initial 35/25/20/20 hypothesis.
- Require reliable Purchase CAPI, refund data, and offer-level margin before this level.

### Stop, iterate, and scale rules

- **Do not stop for weak CTR alone** if purchase economics are good.
- **Stop immediately** for policy rejection that reveals a real violation, broken landing, wrong offer, duplicate purchase event, or material claim mismatch.
- **Normal creative stop:** the ad has spent three to five target CACs, has no purchase or a materially worse qualified-funnel rate than peers, and there is no tracking issue.
- **Funnel stop:** landing-view to valid-search rate is materially below sibling funnels with the same traffic quality; inspect load time, message match, and form friction before blaming the ad.
- **Scale:** net CAC is below target, results persist beyond a one-day spike, refund/chargeback quality is acceptable, and delivery is not dominated by existing customers.
- Increase budgets gradually, commonly 15–25% at a time with 48–72 hours between major changes. Treat that as an operating heuristic; larger changes can be tested in a duplicate scaling cell.

## 12. Creative assessment and regeneration plan

### Why the face-led cheating ads deserve priority

They combine three high-attention elements:

- A specific social moment: after midnight, repeated calls, saved-name mismatch.
- A human identity payoff: an attractive fictional adult face and name make the result immediately legible.
- A concrete product action: check the number.

The face is not enough by itself. A strong variant must show the transition from the number to a reviewable Anya result. The test should compare face-led and product-led executions under the same hook and funnel.

### Current weak points

1. The ten gendered ads reuse one fictional woman and one fictional man, creating fatigue and making all hooks feel like the same ad.
2. HER-04 and HIM-04 ask a broad cheating question that Anya cannot answer directly.
3. Several product panels contain more detail than a phone-feed viewer can absorb.
4. The library is almost entirely polished single-frame creative; it lacks native social proof, simple “ugly” statics, creator explanation, and motion.
5. Parent and trust ads often lack a human focal point while the relationship ads have one.
6. Current destinations do not preserve the visual and verbal promise.
7. Square variants exist only for selected winners; 9:16 adaptations are not yet a complete system.

2. Question hook versus observation hook.
3. Full result panel versus one decisive card.
4. Polished Anya frame versus simple social-native static.

Do not test face, hook, result density, CTA, and offer simultaneously and call the winner a lesson.

### Approval checklist for every creative

- Real Anya logo, not a generic symbol.
- Main hook readable at actual mobile-feed size.
- Fictional adult result data and visible FICTIONAL DEMO label.
- No child face, mugshot, private address, real phone/email, or actual search subject.
- No claim that someone cheated, lied, is dangerous, is a predator, or is a criminal.
- No racialized suspicion or targeting.
- Product input, proof, and CTA agree with the landing funnel.
- All platform/source availability claims are live and supportable.
- Paid-safe copy avoids direct assertions about the viewer's private situation.
- 4:5, 1:1, and 9:16 have intentional compositions rather than crops.

## 15. Metrics and diagnostic scorecard

### Metric hierarchy

#### Business truth

- Server-confirmed gross revenue.
- Net revenue after refunds and chargebacks.
- Variable source/provider cost.
- Contribution margin.
- New-customer CAC.
- 30-, 60-, and 90-day retained subscription value.
- One-time to Starter upgrade rate.

#### Acquisition

- Spend, impressions, reach, frequency, CPM.
- Outbound click-through rate, not all-click CTR.
- Outbound CPC.
- Landing page views and click-to-LPV rate.
- Purchases, cost per purchase, and purchase value.
- Meta-reported ROAS and blended paid ROAS, kept separate.

#### Funnel

- Landing → FunnelStart.
- FunnelStart → valid clue.
- Valid clue → real search started.
- Search started → real preview rendered.
- Preview → save/account.
- Preview → paywall view.
- Paywall → InitiateCheckout.
- Checkout → confirmed Purchase.
- Time to preview and time to purchase.
- Exit rate by step and error code.

#### Quality and risk

- Refund rate by campaign, ad, funnel, and offer.
- Chargeback rate and stated reason.
- Cancellation in 0–1, 2–7, 8–30, and 31–90 days.
- Support tickets per 100 purchases.
- No-result purchase rate and refund rate.
- Ad rejection, negative feedback, hide/report rate, and comment sentiment.
- Data deletion/consent requests and SLA.

### Diagnostic tree

| Symptom                                | Most likely layer                | Check before changing targeting                                            |
| -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| High CPM, low reach                    | Auction/creative/account quality | Rejections, negative feedback, audience size, placement compatibility      |
| Good impressions, poor outbound CTR    | Hook/proof                       | Feed-size readability, relevance, first visual, claim clarity              |
| Good clicks, poor LPV                  | Page delivery                    | Load errors, redirects, mobile speed, consent interstitial, broken UTMs    |
| Good LPV, poor FunnelStart             | Message match                    | Exact hook continuity, CTA, privacy anxiety, wrong clue type               |
| Good start, poor valid clue            | Form                             | Input format, country selector, unnecessary choices, keyboard type         |
| Good search, poor preview              | Product/provider                 | Search latency, zero result, provider errors, source promise mismatch      |
| Good preview, poor checkout            | Offer/trust                      | What is locked, one-time clarity, price, billing cadence, account friction |
| Good checkout, poor Purchase           | Payment                          | Provider handoff, mobile wallet/card options, auth, errors, return route   |
| Good Purchase, high refund             | Promise/quality                  | Ad claim, no-result handling, recurring disclosure, result usefulness      |
| Good Meta ROAS, poor blended economics | Attribution/quality              | Existing-customer share, view-through credit, refunds, duplicate events    |

### How to establish thresholds

Do not import a universal “good CTR” or “good CPA” from a YouTube video. During the first four weeks:

1. Build Anya's baseline by category, placement, age band, funnel, and offer.
2. Compare siblings that had the same objective, offer, time window, and traffic stage.
3. Use target CAC as the hard economic anchor.
4. Promote a metric threshold to the operating rules only after it predicts purchases or quality for more than one test.

## 16. Daily, weekly, and monthly operating rhythm

### Daily launch checks

- Spend and delivery against plan.
- New rejection, warning, payment, domain, or dataset issue.
- Purchase count compared with server truth.
- Broken landing/search/checkout errors.
- Comments containing doxxing, threats, child data, or material confusion.
- Material spend concentration on one creative before judging the whole cell.

Do not rewrite the account every morning. Correct failures; let valid tests run.

### Twice-weekly optimization

- Review ads with enough spend relative to target CAC.
- Inspect the full funnel, not just ad metrics.
- Pause a loser only under the written rules.
- Add at most one or two new creative concepts per ad set per planned cycle.
- Record what changed and why.
- Verify that the landing and creative version still match.

### Weekly business review

- Net CAC and contribution by category/ad/funnel/offer.
- Purchase-event reconciliation and CAPI diagnostics.
- Refund, chargeback, cancellation, and support reasons.
- New versus existing customer share.
- Creative fatigue: frequency, declining net conversion, negative feedback.
- Source reliability and average search cost.
- Next week's one most important creative question and one funnel question.

### Monthly review

- Compare month to month and, when enough history exists, year-over-year seasonality.
- Update allowable CAC from actual retention and margin.
- Run a formal A/B or lift study if volume supports it.
- Refresh winning concepts without abandoning the message that made them work.
- Review Meta policies, FTC/subscription developments, and state-law counsel notes.
- Archive conclusions in this document's change log.

## 17. Policy, legal, and ethical guardrails

### Meta creative and landing review

Meta's review can include images, copy, targeting, positioning, and landing content. The destination must work, represent the advertised product, and comply with the standards.

For Anya:

- Avoid asserting or implying a private fact about the viewer: “Your partner is cheating,” “Your child is talking to a predator,” or “Your email was stolen.”
- Use a question or observation plus a truthful action: “A number keeps showing up after midnight. See what publicly connects.”
- Avoid shocking, sexual, humiliating, or fear-gore imagery.
- Do not use fake buttons, fake search states, or nonexistent features.
- Do not cloak or change the landing content presented to review systems.
- Schedule review time before launch and use Business Support Home for legitimate appeals.

### Subscription and negative-option principles

As of the research date, the FTC's 2024 click-to-cancel rule had been vacated, and the FTC opened a new 2026 rulemaking process. Existing Section 5, ROSCA, state laws, and active enforcement still make the practical standard clear:

- Disclose all material terms clearly before billing information.
- Obtain express informed consent.
- Do not misrepresent price, renewal, savings, results, or cancellation.
- Provide a simple cancellation mechanism.
- Do not make a cancellation survey mandatory.
- Keep proof of the terms and consent version accepted by each customer.

See the [FTC's 2026 negative-option overview](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2026/03/do-you-have-thoughts-negative-option-related-regulations-share-them-ftc) and [2026 enforcement allegations against hidden recurring charges](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2026/06/ftc-sues-stop-sprawling-enterprise-operating-unlawful-subscription-schemes). This section is operational guidance, not legal advice.

### FCRA and high-stakes use

Do not market Anya for employment, housing, credit, insurance, or another regulated eligibility decision unless counsel has designed a compliant consumer-reporting program. FTC guidance states that public records and compiled background data can be consumer reports when used for those purposes.

Important consequence: “not a consumer reporting agency” text does not rescue a product that is actually marketed or supplied for hiring decisions. TRU-01 and TRU-02 therefore remain on hold.

### Parent safety

- Target adults, never minors.
- Do not collect child photos, names, schools, locations, or chat logs for a basic identity lookup.
- Do not imply a person is a predator or offender from an account match.
- Explain that public identity signals can be incomplete or wrong.
- Provide neutral safety resources without manufacturing urgency.

### Relationship safety

- Never claim Anya accesses private messages.
- Never claim a dating profile is currently active unless the source supports activity and the product accurately labels its confidence and timestamp.
- Never infer cheating from a linked account.
- Avoid emotional questions that pressure users to confirm suspicion.
- Include a calm exit or different-clue path when there is no result.

### Generated faces and testimonials

- Use adults only and label the result FICTIONAL DEMO.
- Do not represent a generated face or story as a real customer, partner, or discovered person.
- Keep records of generation provenance and commercial-use rights.
- Review Meta's current AI-ad transparency behavior before launch; Meta has expanded labeling for ads created or significantly edited with generative AI.

## 19. QA scripts and acceptance criteria

### Test identities

- Use reserved example phone numbers, example.com emails, and explicitly fictional usernames.
- Never test ads or screenshots with a real person's phone, email, Discord ID, child identity, or address.
- Maintain deterministic fixtures for 0, 1, 2–5, and 6+ result buckets.

### End-to-end cases

1. Each ad ID opens the correct funnel, hook, logo, clue type, and offer.
2. Meta/UTM parameters persist through purchase without subject data.
3. Back/forward, refresh, tab close, and 24-hour resume work without exposing the clue.
4. Invalid input explains the issue without humiliating or alarming the user.
5. Search with no result never becomes “match found.”
6. Provider timeout does not sell a nonexistent report.
7. Account creation preserves the vault without rerunning a paid provider.
8. Checkout success delivers exactly once.
9. Duplicate webhook does not duplicate Purchase or entitlement.
10. Refund updates the ledger and downstream net-revenue dashboard.
11. Subscription cancellation is reachable and understandable on mobile.
12. Consent denial does not break basic product use where law requires an alternative.

### Meta Events Manager acceptance

- Browser/server event names and IDs deduplicate.
- Purchase value and currency match the payment ledger.
- No Test Events warning for missing critical parameters.
- Event match quality uses only lawful customer data.
- No searched clue appears in event payload inspection.
- Development transactions are excluded from production optimization.
